Question 

*What lessons can be learnt from the history of the home video game industry that have been used to help launch the Sony PlayStation II and Microsoft’s X-Box? Do Microsoft and Sony appear to have learnt and applied these lessons?* 

When Sony and Microsoft launched the Playstation 2 and Xbox in the early 2000s there was already half a century of experience to learn from and use. Both the PS2 and the Xbox are showing how they clearly applied previous lessons learnt from predecessors like Atari, Nintendo, Sega and 3DO.

# Lessons 

* Quality control and game library (lessons from Atari and Nintendo)
* * Sony realised the importance of a strong supply for good games. The PS2 was compatible with PS1 games which meant it already had 100s of games available at launch. This was a lesson learnt from the Atari crash and Nintendo strategy of control over game libraries.

  * Microsoft saw the same need and went hard on exclusive games like Halo to attract buyers. 
* Strong franchises and Third Party development (lessons from Nintendo and Sony PS1)
* * Sony had already built strong relationships with multiple third party license developers when focusing on PS1. This deepened during the PS2 which resulted in a multitude of game releases.

  * Microsoft, who was new to the console market bought their own game studios and worked to build relationships with third party license developers. This shows that they understood the weight of good games and is why they focused on exclusive franchises as a driving force for sales. They were also not completely new to the game industry since they already had released popular PC games which gave them confidence in game development.
* Marketing and Positioning (lesson from Sega)
* * Sega showed how targeted marketing could drive success by focusing on more graphics games for an older audiance. Sony also applied this and positioned the PS2 as more than only a videogame console. Their marketing also emphasized its role as an affordable DVD player. This attracted a broader and older audience.

  * Microsoft targeted a similar group and above all an older consumer that wanted more realism and technically advanced games. Xbox built the most powerful console on the market which also was appealing to more technically interested consumers.
* Pricing and availability (lesson from 3DOs failure)
* * 3DOs fall showed that too expensive hardware and a lack of games made consumers lose interest in buying the console. Sony made sure not to make the same mistake and priced the PS2 at a reasonable price which made it very attractive given its DVD functionalities. This made the customers feel like they got more than a console for the money.

  * Microsoft made the same decision and even lost money on the hardware in the beginning to build trust and a customer base, this meant that they had to make their money on selling games in the beginning.
* Technology and longevity(lesson from Nintendo and Sony PS1)
* * When Nintendo introduced their battery-backed cartridges they showed how technology advancements both became a selling point and introduced longevity to their console. Sony applied the same mind set when they decided to build on the PS1’s use of CD-ROM, Sony advanced further by adopting DVDs, which allowed better graphics. This made the console a multimedia center which gave it a unique advantage against the competitors.

  * Microsoft launched Xbox Live, an early investment in online games. This shows that they not only learned from history but also sought to create a new direction for the industry.

# Summary

Both Sony and Microsoft clearly applied lessons from earlier generations when launching their consoles. Sony positioned the PS2 as both a gaming system and a multimedia device, using DVD functionality and backward compatibility to attract a wide audience and ensure a strong game library from day one. Microsoft entering the market for the first time, took a different but clever approach by building the most powerful console available and investing heavily in exclusive titles and Xbox Live, They even accepted hardware losses to gain customer trust, which i see as a smart long term strategy since they were new and did not have a customer base. Both companies also relied on exclusive franchises to create loyal fanbases and establish their place in the industry.
